Get your saves into Atlas

Two short guides: export your saved posts from Instagram, then put them to work in Atlas.

Part One

Export your saved posts

Instagram won't hand your saves to Atlas directly — you first ask Meta for a copy of your data, wait for it to be prepared, then download it. Follow these seven steps in order.

Heads up: Meta doesn't prepare this instantly. Depending on load, it can take anywhere from a few minutes to several hours. You can close the tab and come back later.
01
Open the Accounts Center

Everything in this guide happens on one Meta page — the same one you'll return to later to download your file. Open it now:

Open Accounts Center →

Bookmark this tab. Step 7 sends you right back here.

02
Choose "Some of your information"

On the Accounts Center page, select Download or transfer information, choose your Instagram account, then pick Some of your information — not "All available information". You only need one category.

03
Select only "Saved posts"

Use the search box to find Saved posts and tick just that one. Leave everything else unchecked — it keeps the export small and ready sooner.

04
Choose "Download to device"

When asked where to send the export, pick Download to device — not "Transfer to another destination". Atlas reads a local file, so the export needs to land on your computer.

05
Set date range, format, and media quality
  • Date range: All time
  • Format: JSON (preferred — HTML also works, but Atlas parses JSON more reliably)
  • Media quality: Low — Atlas doesn't use the downloaded media files, only the post data

Confirm the request when ready.

06
Wait for Meta to prepare your file

This is the slow part — anywhere from a few minutes to several hours, depending on how much Meta has to process.

If you chose email notifications, Meta will email you when it's ready — that email contains the download instructions and a link back to this same page.
07
Download, extract, and locate the file

Return to the Accounts Center page — the export will now show as ready to download:

Open Accounts Center →
accountscenter.instagram.com
Saved posts export Ready to download
Download

Download the ZIP file and extract it. Inside, find:

📁 your_instagram_activity/ 📁 saved/ 📄 saved_posts.json
Import the extracted file, not the ZIP. Atlas reads saved_posts.json (or .html) directly. Importing merges with what's already in Atlas — it never replaces your stars, tags, or notes, and re-importing later is always safe.
Part Two

Using Atlas

Once your file is imported, here's what Atlas does with it.

Import

Reads the extracted saved_posts.json (or .html) and merges it into your library — matching by post, so nothing is duplicated.

Toolbar → Import

Card grid

Every saved post as a card. Click anywhere on a card to open its full detail view.

Click any card

Type filters

Narrow the grid to just Reels or just Posts.

Toolbar segment: All / Reels / Posts

Search

Full-text search across captions and creator names.

Toolbar search box

Sort

Order the grid by newest, oldest, or creator A–Z.

Toolbar sort dropdown

Sidebar scopes

Jump straight to All Saves, ★ Favorites, Watched, Untagged, or any single tag.

Sidebar → Library

Tags

Create custom tags with their own colors. Assign them from the post modal or filter by clicking a tag chip anywhere.

Modal → tag combobox, or sidebar → + new tag

Notes

Free-text notes attached to any post — for context only you need.

Post modal → Notes

Post modal

A split view with the live Instagram embed alongside caption, owner link, collections, tags, and notes.

Click a card to open

Stats strip

Running totals across your library: saved, reels, collections, creators, favorites, watched.

Top of the page, always visible

Favorites / Watched

Star a post to favorite it, or mark it done once you've watched or read it.

Card or modal → star / check icons

Trash

Deleting a post moves it to Trash, where it's restorable. Emptying Trash is the only destructive action in Atlas — and purged posts stay gone even if you re-import.

Sidebar → Trash

Reset

A full, explicit wipe of everything Atlas has stored, for when you want to start clean.

Toolbar → Reset
Everything above lives entirely in your browser's local storage (IndexedDB). Nothing you import, tag, or note ever leaves your machine.
